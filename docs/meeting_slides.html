Dose-Budgeted Multi-Robot Source Search

Can a robot team learn to find a lost radioactive source without any robot exceeding its own radiation budget?

The problem

The best information is where the danger is

  • Robots are sent in instead of people, but radiation destroys them too. Fukushima robot cameras and lidar failed at about 120 Gy.
  • Close readings locate the source best, but dose grows like 1/distance².

Research questions

Three questions

RQ1 · Safety

Can a constrained learning method keep the team within budget in ≥ 95% of missions, losing at most 5 points of success? Does it beat a hand-designed safe planner?

RQ2 · Constraint vs penalty

Does a constraint hit the safety target across budgets and source strengths with one setting, where a dose penalty needs re-tuning?

RQ3 · Risk sharing

With unequal budgets, does the team give the risky close readings to robots that can afford them?

Approach

How we plan to solve it

Simulator

20 × 20 m room, walls, 3–6 robots, standard radiation physics with realistic detector error

Shared estimate

Particle filter: thousands of guesses of source position and strength

Learning team

One shared policy (MAPPO); a safety price λ for each robot

Safety backup

Shield that blocks moves predicted to exceed the budget

Evaluation

Same 1,000 test missions for every method, 10 seeds, confidence intervals

Compared with five hand-designed strategies and a fixed-penalty version. Status: everything built and tested (55 tests); only short single training runs so far.

First results

What we found in the first tests

96%

Going close helps find the source

When at least one robot got within 2 m of the source, the team found it in 96% of missions. When no robot went that close, it found it in only 70%.

1 robot

The team sends one robot in

Nobody told it to, but the trained team usually sends only one robot close while the others stay back. A simple rule that sends everyone in sends about three robots, and some robot goes over budget in 47% of missions.

4 in 10

That robot often gets stuck

With very strong sources (30–100 TBq), about 4 in 10 robots that go close use up their whole dose budget next to the source and have to stop there.

These are early numbers from one short training run per method and 200 test missions. They show the system works; they are not final results.

Why, and what next

The robot follows the rules we gave it

The mission ends once the source is found, so stranding a robot on the last step costs almost nothing. The rules don't yet say that stranding is bad.

  • Redefine the task: a robot that can't get back out counts as a failure, so it has to keep budget for the exit.
  • Activate the safety constraint: limit the chance of exceeding the budget, and split the team's 5% across robots (fixed, needs a run).
  • Give robots a predicted-dose reading for their next moves, and focus experiments on strong sources (10–100 TBq).
  • Run the full experiments on the cluster: about 370 training runs.
